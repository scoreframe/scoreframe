Read this first

Anyone could add themselves to a private team Found + closed today

The rule that let a person create their own follower record put no constraint on status. So any signed-in user could write {status:"approved"} onto any private team and immediately read its roster — and, because surname access trusts "approved follower", the players' full names.

Verified against a throwaway private team: the read was refused, the self-approve succeeded, the read then worked. Now refused, and re-verified after the fix — while a genuine request-to-follow and public auto-follow both still work. Accepting an invite moved to the server, which checks the invite really was sent to your address. iOS 1.2.214 is required for invite acceptance; older builds will fail at it.

Surnames are out of the public data Yes — informational

You asked what this meant: a shared team's records used to be readable by anyone, so a child's full name was one fetch away even though the page only drew "Hayden H." Surnames now live in a separate place only staff and approved followers of a private team can read, and a server rule enforces it on every write. So — done, and it matches what you want.

An approved family link now grants authority Your call, built

Approving a link now records a verified guardian relationship the permission system can act on, instead of only flipping a status nothing read.

Mapped conservatively: parent and guardian become custodial; grandparent and other family stop at "can see this player". That reads "parent or guardian" literally — custodial authority following the custodial relationship. Easy to widen when you decide what those people should be able to do; much harder to walk back.

Fixed the last two days

Invitations you can act on without the email Verified in a browser

Inviting a ScoreFrame user used to make them a follower on the spot. It now creates a pending follow and the invitation waits on their dashboard with Accept / No thanks — checked by signing in as the invited account and reading the rendered page, which shows "You've been invited to follow PVA 14-1 Select". The email is a nudge; the dashboard is the durable surface.

Roster order, and who runs the team

The web roster now appears in the order you set it in the app. It had been sorted by document id — UUIDs — because the records the page reads carried no order field at all. Confirmed live as #1 Maebry through #10 Arya.

Followers can also now see the owner, managers and coaches. Names and roles only: no email, no photo. Knowing who runs your kid's team is reasonable; a contact directory for those people is not, and they never agreed to one.

Housekeeping you asked for

Carter J removed. Micha left alone for you to add to ONW. The husk inventory emailed in full — 11 teams, 34 leftover documents, 15 game records with dates, sports and scores — with nothing deleted.

Editing a player didn't reach anything

Add a player as "Audrey", add the surname later, and the roster kept showing "Audrey" — the roster stored its own copy at add time and nothing refreshed it. The player now owns their name; number and position stay per-team, because the same kid really does wear 7 here and 12 there. Then the same staleness turned up in the cloud, so the web page still showed the old name: renaming now pushes to every team they're on, and your existing teams have been repaired.

Removing a player from a roster

The ✕ deleted immediately and permanently, on a screen with a Save button. It now asks, says what it costs, and waits for Save — marked rows stay visible, struck through, with undo. Removing the last player also never reached the cloud at all: the sweep that infers removals is guarded against stale caches, so deliberate removal now names the player instead of being inferred.

"Anonymous" followers

The email invite looked the person up by address and then never saved their name. Fixed at the invite, fixed when reading, and your existing followers repaired.

Web: unreadable box, signed-out top bar, mobile layout on desktop

The Find Teams description used a variable that doesn't exist, so it fell through to the browser's visited-link purple — and even corrected, that grey measured 2.5:1 against the panel, so it changed colour as well. The account bar showed "Sign In" after a Back because the repaint only ran once the team document had loaded; it now repaints on every auth event and on a bfcache restore. And above 900px the pages are a centred 760px column instead of stretching edge to edge.

What the numbers actually say

"10 new users" was 2

7 were automated signups matching name.NNNNN@gmail.com — they authenticate against Firebase Auth directly, never open the app, and write nothing. 3 were anonymous web sessions from the scoring page, which were never signups. The digest read a collection nothing had written in months; it now reads Auth itself and labels every account. Over 30 days: 39 accounts, 4 recorded a game.

59% of push tokens are dead — and that's churn, not a bug

41 of 99 are deliverable. Dead rate tracks time since last app open (33% within 7 days → 68% past 90), which is the shape of uninstalls. The token refresh path was already correct. A failed send now stamps uninstalledAt so it reads as a retention metric. This is why the email matters: it reaches people the app can't.
