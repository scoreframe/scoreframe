Privacy Policy

Effective [EFFECTIVE DATE] · Last updated [DATE]

KinCamp is a private space for your family: chat, plans, meals, lists, a family tree and a few games. Families trust us with a lot, including information about their kids. This policy explains, in plain words, what we collect, why, who can see it, and what you can do about it.

The short version

  • Your family's information is for your family. What you put in a camp is visible to the members of that camp, not to the public.
  • We don't sell your information, we don't show third-party ads, and we don't track you across other apps or websites.
  • Location is your choice. Sharing where you are with your family is off until you turn it on, on your own phone. We keep only your latest spot, not a history.
  • Kids are welcome, with a parent in charge. Children under 13 use KinCamp only with a parent's permission, and parents can review or delete their child's information.
  • Some features use outside services, like AI that suggests dinners or writes your weekly recap, and weather and maps services. We share only what each feature needs.
  • You can delete your account from inside the app at any time.

1. Who we are

KinCamp is made by Glavin Labs LLC ("we", "us"). This policy covers the KinCamp apps for iPhone and Android and the kincamp.app website.

A few KinCamp words you'll see below:

  • Camp: a private family group. You can belong to more than one.
  • Leader: a parent or other adult who runs a camp, invites people and manages settings.
  • Camper: a full member of a camp, such as a parent, teen or child.
  • Visitor: extended family, like grandparents or cousins, with limited, mostly read-only access.

2. What we collect

Your account

  • How you sign in. Your phone number (if you sign in with a text code), or the name, email address and profile photo that Apple or Google shares when you sign in with them. If you use Apple's "Hide My Email", we get a private relay address instead of your real one.
  • Your display name and profile photo, which you can change.
  • Your settings, such as notification choices, quiet hours and how you arrange the app.

Your profile ("About me")

Everything here is optional: your birthday (you can hide the birth year), clothing and shoe sizes, interests, favorite foods, candy, places to eat and activities, hobbies, and a short "about me". It helps your family with birthdays and gifts. You can also set an "Away from camp" status, such as away at college, with a short note on where and the dates you'll be home.

What your family creates together

Camp chat and direct messages, lists and groceries, chores and rewards, calendar events, meals, recipes and restaurants, the pantry, gift lists and who has claimed a gift, Table Talk answers, game plays and scores, and the family tree. The tree can include people who don't use KinCamp, such as grandparents or relatives who have passed away, with details like names, dates and places of birth and death, gender, occupation, stories and photos.

Photos

Photos you choose to add: your profile photo, photos of family members in the tree, and photo proof for a finished chore. When you snap or pick a photo of a recipe card, it's sent to our AI provider to read the recipe and isn't stored as a photo. KinCamp asks for camera or photo library access only when you use one of these features.

Location (only if you allow it)

  • Weather where you are. If you let KinCamp use your location, it saves a spot rounded to about 1 km (about half a mile), plus the nearby town name and time zone, so it can show your local forecast and severe weather warnings. Only you can see this spot. You can turn it off in Settings.
  • Restaurants nearby. When you ask for restaurant ideas, your current location is used for that search.
  • Places (family location sharing). If you turn it on, your phone shares your latest location with the campers and leaders in your camp. See your choices for the details.
  • Your camp's home location. A leader can set the camp's ZIP code, which we turn into a town and coordinates for the camp's weather and local news.

Step counts (only if you join)

If you join the family step challenge, KinCamp reads your daily step total from Apple Health or Health Connect and shares that one number per day with your camp. It reads nothing else from your health data and never writes to it.

Your voice (only when you tap the mic)

You can dictate instead of typing in Ask camp, chat and Table Talk. Your phone's built-in speech service (from Apple or Google) turns your words into text. KinCamp receives only the text. We never receive or store a recording of your voice.

Device and app information

  • A notification token so we can send you notifications.
  • A random device ID that KinCamp creates, plus your phone's model name and type. Your account is signed in on one phone at a time, and this is how we know which phone that is.
  • Crash reports and basic app-usage information, through Google's Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics, so we can fix bugs and see which features are used.
  • Security checks. Google and Apple may check that sign-in requests come from a real copy of the app on a real device.

Purchases

If you buy KinCamp Pro, Apple or Google handles the payment. We never see your card number. We receive what we need to give your camp Pro: the plan, its status, renewal and expiry dates, which store you used, and a purchase reference from the store.

When you contact us

If you email us, we keep your message and our reply so we can help you.

Connected calendars and links

  • Google Calendar. If you connect it, we get read-only access to the calendars you pick, plus your Google email address so you can tell accounts apart. We copy those events into KinCamp. We never change your Google Calendar. KinCamp's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.
  • Calendar feeds. If you add a calendar web address (an ICS or webcal link), our servers fetch it to copy the events.
  • Links you paste. When you add a product link to a gift list or a recipe link, our servers visit that page to grab a title, picture and price, or the recipe.

3. How we use it

  • To run KinCamp: show your family's chat, lists, calendar, meals, tree and games to the right people, and keep them in sync.
  • To send the notifications you've asked for, like chat messages, the Family Bell, chore reminders, birthday nudges, weather warnings, your Sunday Campfire recap and place arrivals.
  • To power features you choose to use, such as AI dinner ideas, Ask camp, recipe import, weather and news.
  • To keep accounts safe, keep you signed in on one phone at a time, and prevent abuse.
  • To fix crashes and improve the app.
  • To manage KinCamp Pro.
  • To answer you when you contact us, and to follow the law.

We don't use your information for advertising, and we don't build profiles of you for anyone else.

6. AI features

Some KinCamp features use Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. Anthropic processes this information to provide the feature to us. Under its commercial terms, it doesn't use it to train its AI models. We don't use your information to train AI models either.

Here's what each feature sends:

  • Ask camp: what you typed or said, plus the names of your camp members as they appear in the app, your list names and your saved meal names, so it can figure out "remind Mia about soccer Thursday".
  • Sunday Campfire recap: a summary of your family's week, including event titles and times, upcoming birthdays and ages, the names of people involved, favorite meals and list counts. It may include events synced from Google Calendar.
  • Dinner ideas, recipes and grocery aisles: your saved meals, ratings, pantry items, grocery item names, and any request you typed.
  • Restaurant ideas: your saved restaurants, how many people favorited each, nearby restaurants from Google, your location at the time of the search, and your request.
  • Recipe import: the recipe photo, link or text you give it.
  • Camp Daily, Table Talk and trivia: public news headlines, and nothing about your family.

AI can make mistakes. Always double-check recipes for allergies and anything that matters.

7. Things we never do

  • We never sell your personal information, or "share" it for cross-context behavioral advertising as California law defines it.
  • We don't show third-party ads. The only promotions in KinCamp are for KinCamp Pro itself.
  • We don't track you across other companies' apps or websites.
  • We don't use health data (step counts) for advertising, and we never share it outside your camp except with the service providers that run KinCamp.
  • We don't keep a history of where you've been.
  • We don't read your private messages, except when you ask us to help with a problem or when the law requires it.

8. Children under 13

KinCamp is made for whole families, so children are part of it. A U.S. law called COPPA (the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act) protects children under 13. Here's how we follow it.

A parent is always in charge

A child under 13 can join KinCamp only after a parent or legal guardian gives permission through our parent consent step. If we learn we've collected information from a child under 13 without a parent's permission, we'll delete it.

What we collect from or about a child

Only what the family features need:

  • a sign-in method;
  • a name and photo;
  • an optional profile (birthday, sizes, favorites);
  • what they post in chat, lists, chores and games;
  • chore photos;
  • a notification token and device ID.

Some features are off for children until a parent turns them on: location sharing (Places), the step challenge, AI features, and chore photos. We don't collect more than a feature needs, and we never make a child's participation depend on sharing more.

How a child's information is used and shared

We use it only to run KinCamp for the family. It's visible to the members of the child's camp, as described in who can see what. Parents should know that a child can chat with everyone in the camp, including visitors, and can send direct messages to camp members.

We share it only with the service providers listed above, to run the features the parent has allowed. We never sell it, never use it for advertising, and never make it public.

Parents' rights

A parent can, at any time:

  • see the information we have about their child;
  • correct or delete it;
  • stop further collection.

Deleting a child's account removes them from KinCamp. Email support@kincamp.app to do any of these, or use the parent tools in the app. We'll verify that you're the parent before sharing or deleting anything.

We keep a child's information only as long as their account is active and the family uses it. We delete it as described in how long we keep things.

Questions about a child's privacy? Contact Glavin Labs LLC, [MAILING ADDRESS], [PHONE], support@kincamp.app.

9. Teens

Teens 13 to 17 can have their own account, with a parent's or guardian's permission. We treat teen accounts carefully:

  • Location sharing is always the teen's own choice, made on their own phone. A parent can't turn it on for them.
  • Direct messages stay private between the two people in them.
  • Teens are on the "kids" team in games. By default, they see only good-news headlines unless a leader changes that.
  • We never sell teens' information or use it for advertising.

10. Your choices and controls

Location sharing (Places)

  • It's off until you turn it on, on your own phone. Nobody can turn it on for you.
  • When it's on, campers and leaders in your camp see your latest spot on a family map. They can choose to get a notification when you arrive at or leave a place the family has saved, like "School" or "Home".
  • We keep only your latest spot, replacing it as you move. We don't keep a history of where you've been. Turning sharing off deletes your spot.
  • You can share just your city instead of your exact spot, pause sharing for a while, or share only for a trip that ends on its own.
  • "On my way" posts a message in camp chat with your estimated arrival time, and a "Made it" message when you arrive.
  • Your phone checks in only when you move a meaningful distance or reach a saved place, not constantly.

Other controls

  • Phone permissions. Location, camera, photos, microphone, health data and notifications can each be turned off in your phone's settings.
  • Notifications. Choose which you get and set quiet hours in Settings.
  • Weather location. Turn off "weather where I am" in Settings.
  • Step challenge. Leave the step challenge any time. You can also revoke access in Apple Health or Health Connect.
  • Profile. Edit or clear your profile any time, and hide your birth year.
  • Google Calendar. Disconnect it in KinCamp, or remove KinCamp's access in your Google Account.
  • Leaving a camp. You can leave a camp. Your personal calendars and weather data for that camp are deleted, and you're removed from its location sharing.
  • Your posts. You can delete your own chat messages and the items you created.

11. Deleting your account

You can delete your account in the app's settings (in the Ranger Station). When you do, we:

  • remove you from every camp;
  • delete your member profile in each camp;
  • delete your personal calendars, weather data and location data;
  • disconnect any Google Calendar you connected, and delete the family calendars synced through it;
  • delete your account record and your sign-in.

Some things you added stay with the family, because they're part of shared family records:

  • chat messages you didn't delete;
  • list items, meals and events you added;
  • family tree entries.

Delete what you want removed before you go, or ask us. If you're the only leader of a camp, we'll make the longest-standing camper a leader, or ask you to choose one first.

If you have KinCamp Pro, deleting your account doesn't cancel your subscription. Cancel it in the App Store or Google Play.

You can also email support@kincamp.app to ask us to delete your account or specific information.

13. Keeping it safe

We protect your information in several ways:

  • Encrypted in transit: information is encrypted while it travels.
  • Google infrastructure: it's stored on Google Cloud systems, which encrypt data at rest.
  • Access rules: server-side rules decide who can read each piece of information, so a camp's data is available only to that camp's members.
  • Limited staff access: only a small number of our people can reach your data, and only to run and support KinCamp.
  • Plan: we keep a written information security program.

No system is perfectly secure. If we learn of a breach that affects you, we'll tell you as the law requires.

14. Your privacy rights (including California)

Wherever you live, you can ask to see, correct, download or delete your personal information. Email support@kincamp.app. We'll confirm it's really you, usually by having you write from the email tied to your account or confirm in the app, and we'll respond within 45 days. If we can't do something you asked, we'll explain why, and you can ask us to reconsider. We won't treat you differently for using your rights.

California residents

The California Consumer Privacy Act, as amended, gives you these rights:

  • Know: what we collect, use and disclose.
  • Delete: have it deleted.
  • Correct: fix it if it's wrong.
  • Opt out: of the sale or sharing of your information. We don't sell or share it.
  • Limit: our use of sensitive information. We use it only to provide the service.
  • Equal treatment: no penalty for using these rights.

An authorized agent can make a request for you with your signed permission.

In the last 12 months we collected these categories, from you, your family members and your device:

  • identifiers, such as name, email, phone number, account ID and device ID;
  • personal records, such as name and phone number;
  • characteristics, such as age, birthday and, in the family tree, gender;
  • commercial information, such as your Pro subscription;
  • app activity, such as how you use the app and crash data;
  • geolocation, if you allow it;
  • photos;
  • sensitive information: precise location, the contents of messages, account sign-in, and step counts.

We use them for the purposes in section 3 and disclose them only to the service providers in section 5. We do not sell or share personal information, including that of consumers under 16. California's "Shine the Light" law: we don't share personal information with others for their direct marketing.

Other U.S. states

Many states give residents similar rights, including Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas, Oregon and others: to access, correct, delete and get a copy of your information, to opt out of targeted advertising, sale and profiling, and to appeal our decision. We honor these requests for everyone. To appeal a decision, reply to our response with "Appeal" in the subject line. If we deny your appeal, you can contact your state's attorney general.

Health data (step counts)

Some states, including Washington and Nevada, have special laws for consumer health data. We collect step counts only if you join the step challenge. We use them only for the challenge, share them only with your camp and our service providers, and never sell them. You can leave the challenge and ask us to delete your step history at any time.

KinCamp is run from the United States, and your information is stored and processed in the U.S. KinCamp is meant for families in the U.S. Phone sign-in is available only for U.S. numbers.
